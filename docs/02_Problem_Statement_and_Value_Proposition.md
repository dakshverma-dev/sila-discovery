# Problem Statement and Value Proposition


D02 | Version 0.1 | 12 October 2026


State the failure to investigate and the proposed value for each party.


Status: proposed implementation specification. Application acceptance tests remain pending.


## Problem statement

A community organiser may find a specialist cultural session attractive but unaffordable at their group size. Another community may face the same constraint without knowing a shared booking is possible. A venue needs enough revenue or attendance to justify delivery, but assembling suitable groups can require repeated conversations about subject, timing, price and sharing.

## Observed basis and hypotheses

| Status | Claim | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed precedent | Private cultural experiences can have fixed hosting fees. | Published venue packages establish the pricing mechanism, not unmet demand. |
| Hypothesis | Several small groups would prefer sharing to abandoning a booking. | Interview organisers about their last failed booking; test actual sharing consent. |
| Hypothesis | Venues would pay for incremental bookings. | Agree a pilot fee or written willingness to repeat. |
| Hypothesis | Qloo finds useful connections organisers miss. | Blind comparison with organiser and curator recommendations. |

## Value proposition

We help a venue and several small communities agree a shared cultural experience, check its economics and practical conditions, and collect reservations that make a booking possible. The venue remains responsible for delivery and final confirmation.

| Customer or user | Current workaround | Proposed value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venue | Public listings, group enquiries, manual coordination. | A structured route to additional specialist bookings. |
| Organiser | WhatsApp discussions, direct enquiries, cancelling or paying more. | Partner discovery, clear proposals and less chasing. |
| Participant | Pay for a small private group, join a public event, or do nothing. | Share eligible hosting costs while retaining their own group connections. |

## Discovery questions

- Describe a real outing you abandoned or scaled back. What stopped it?
- Would you share with another group? Under what conditions would you refuse?
- What staff time and minimum revenue does the venue require?
- Could an ordinary public event achieve the same result with less coordination?
- Who pays for coordination today, and what evidence would justify a fee?

## Positioning and exclusions

The proposed differentiation is combining cultural discovery with venue-constrained coalition formation and reservation coordination. It is not a proven moat. The product should stop when a simpler existing booking option satisfies the request.
